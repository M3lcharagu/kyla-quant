## How to use

Complete the trading plan before risking capital. Use the pre-trade checklist before every order. Size from the stop distance and planned loss, not from buying power. Record facts before changing an order. Review process quality separately from the outcome. Change one rule at a time and keep a large enough sample to learn.

## Pre-trade checklist

### Hard stops — every answer must be yes

- [ ] Instrument and market are allowed by my written plan.
- [ ] The setup is defined by rules, not a feeling or prediction.
- [ ] The entry trigger is present and timestamped.
- [ ] Invalidation or stop level is defined before entry.
- [ ] Maximum planned loss is within my per-trade risk limit.
- [ ] Size is calculated from stop distance and risk budget.
- [ ] Open-risk and correlation limits remain within plan.
- [ ] Session, spread, liquidity, news, and execution conditions checked.
- [ ] Reward-to-risk remains acceptable after fees and slippage assumptions.
- [ ] I am not revenge trading, chasing, exhausted, intoxicated, or under pressure.
- [ ] I know the rules for stop, target, partials, and early exit.
- [ ] I accept the planned loss without changing rules mid-trade.

### Process questions

- What was the original thesis in one sentence?
- What was known at entry versus learned afterward?
- Which rule did I follow best?
- Which rule did I bend, ignore, or invent?
- Was size correct for the actual stop distance?
- Did an unplanned decision happen? What triggered it?
- What emotion was present and what behavior did it produce?
- What would I repeat next time?
- What one specific adjustment will I test?

## Risk sizing worksheet and formulas

Use units that match your instrument. Confirm contract size, tick or pip value, fees, and platform conventions.

- Account equity: `E = __________`
- Risk percent as a decimal: `r = __________` (example: 0.005 for 0.5%)
- Fixed cash risk: `R = E × r = __________`
- Planned entry: `Pentry = __________`
- Stop or invalidation: `Pstop = __________`
- Stop distance: `D = absolute(Pentry − Pstop) = __________`
- Value per price unit per one unit: `V = __________`
- Estimated round-trip costs: `C = __________`
- Risk per unit: `U = D × V + C = __________`
- Position size: `Q = R ÷ U = __________`
- Round down to the venue’s allowed increment: `Qfinal = __________`
- Actual planned risk: `Qfinal × U = __________`

For percentage-risk sizing: `cash risk = equity × risk percentage`. For a long or short trade, stop distance is the absolute difference between entry and invalidation. If a contract multiplier applies, include it in `V`. If there is a currency conversion, include a conservative conversion assumption. Never use a formula you cannot explain.

### Worked example for arithmetic only

If equity is $10,000, risk is 0.5%, entry is 50, stop is 48, value per price unit is $1, and estimated costs are $2: cash risk is $50; distance is $2; risk per unit is $4; size is 12.5 units, rounded down according to the venue. This is not a recommendation and ignores instrument-specific rules.
