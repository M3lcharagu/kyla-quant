# KYLA Scalper's Discipline Playbook

> **Risk disclaimer:** This playbook is educational material, not financial advice, a trading signal service, or a promise of profit. Trading can lead to loss of capital. The personal setup described here is a process reference—not a target, recommendation, or guarantee. Adapt every rule to your own account, broker or funded-account agreement, risk tolerance, and local requirements.

## 1. The point of this playbook

A scalper does not need more excitement; they need fewer avoidable decisions. This playbook is a compact operating system for showing up, defining risk before entry, limiting exposure, and reviewing behavior without self-judgment.

The KYLA reference setup is:

- **Maximum 3 trades, Monday to Thursday.** Once the limit is reached, the session is over. A missed trade is not a reason to break the limit.
- **Maximum 45-minute hold.** If the trade has not resolved within the planned window, follow your pre-written exit rule rather than negotiating with the chart.
- **One tight per-trade guard.** Define the invalidation or maximum acceptable loss before entry. Place it where your plan says it belongs, never widen it to rescue a losing position, and stop if the guard is hit.
- **A $150/week trailing target reference on a $5,000 funded account.** This is a personal process reference only. It is not a profit promise, a required target, or evidence of likely results. Account rules always come first.
- **A kill switch.** When a hard stop is reached, the platform is closed and the session is logged. Discipline includes stopping.

These constraints are not a strategy or a guarantee. They are guardrails for applying your own tested setup with less impulsive behavior.

## 2. Risk architecture

Before the session, write down what would make you wrong and what would make you stop. A valid plan has an entry reason, an invalidation point, a maximum time in the trade, and a session stop condition.

### Per-trade guard

Your guard is the maximum loss or invalidation point you define before entering. Keep it visible, place it in the platform when possible, and do not move it farther from invalidation. If the guard is hit, record the event; do not immediately re-enter to win it back.

Do not copy a percentage, position size, or dollar amount from someone else. Calculate size from your own account rules and risk tolerance. If a funded account's daily loss limit, drawdown rule, news rule, or other restriction is tighter than this playbook, the funded-account rule wins.

### Weekly reference

The $150 trailing target on a $5,000 funded account is a way to describe the setup's weekly reference point, not a forecast. A week can be flat, negative, or incomplete. Never increase risk just because the reference has not been reached. Protecting the account and following the plan are valid outcomes.

## 3. Kill-switch rules

Write your exact switch conditions in the blank space below before trading:

- Trade-count stop: **3 trades from Monday to Thursday.**
- Guard stop: **A per-trade guard is hit; no revenge entry.**
- Time stop: **The 45-minute maximum hold is reached; follow the planned exit.**
- Behavior stop: **I notice impulsive sizing, moving a guard, chasing, or trading to change my mood.**
- Account stop: **My broker or funded-account limit says stop.**
- Personal stop: **I am too anxious, dysregulated, sleep-deprived, or distracted to execute my checklist.**

**My additional kill-switch condition:** ______________________________________

When a switch triggers: flatten or manage the position according to the pre-written plan, close the platform, write a short log, and take a real break. A stop is a completed decision—not a challenge to negotiate.

## 4. Pre-trade checklist

Copy this block into your journal and complete it before every entry:

- [ ] Is today Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday, and am I still below 3 trades?
- [ ] What is the setup and why is it valid according to my own plan?
- [ ] What would invalidate it?
- [ ] Where is my per-trade guard, and is it placed before entry?
- [ ] What is my maximum hold time? **45 minutes maximum for this reference setup.**
- [ ] Is the position size compatible with my account and funded-account rules?
- [ ] Am I calm enough to follow the plan, or am I chasing relief, excitement, or a loss?
- [ ] What is my exit plan if price moves for me, against me, or nowhere?
- [ ] Have I accepted the possible loss before clicking buy or sell?

If any answer is unclear, the trade is not ready. “No trade” is a complete decision.

## 6. Post-trade review

Answer these after the position is closed, whether it won, lost, or was flat:

1. Did I take a planned setup or manufacture a reason after seeing movement?
2. Was the guard defined and respected before entry?
3. Did I honor the 45-minute maximum hold and the planned exit?
4. Did I follow the trade-count limit and account rules?
5. What did I feel immediately before entry, during the trade, and after exit?
6. Did ADHD or anxiety show up as urgency, distraction, impulsive clicking, avoidance, or rumination?
7. What evidence supports my process assessment—rather than a story based only on the result?
8. What is one small rule or environment change for the next session?
9. Should the kill switch stay on for the rest of the day?

Review behavior first. A rule-following loss can be a good process outcome; a rule-breaking win can hide a dangerous habit.

## 7. Mindset support for ADHD and anxiety

Make the right action easier to start and the wrong action harder to repeat:

- Use one visible checklist, one journal, and one chart layout. Reduce open tabs and notifications.
- Set a timer for the session and a separate 45-minute maximum-hold reminder. Alarms support attention; they are not a substitute for an exit plan.
- Pre-write the guard, exit, and kill-switch condition before the order window opens.
- Use a pause script: **“I can miss this trade. I cannot outsource my risk to urgency.”** Take three slow breaths, then re-read the checklist.
- If anxiety is high or focus is fragmented, step away. No trade is safer than a forced trade.
- After a loss, do not use a new position to change an emotion. Log it, close the platform, and return only when the written conditions allow it.
- Seek qualified mental-health or financial guidance when symptoms or losses are affecting daily life. This playbook is not a treatment plan.

## 8. Weekly reset

At the end of the week, count planned trades, rule-following trades, guard violations, time violations, kill-switch uses, and journal completion. Do not change the system from one exciting result. Change one process variable only after enough honest observations to understand what happened.
